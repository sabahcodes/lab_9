TASK 1 - Estimate Housing Prices Near Transit
==================================================================
Results (414 transactions):
  Slope     = -0.7262 price units per 100 m
  Intercept = 45.8514 price units
  Prediction at 500 m = 42.22 price units (10,000 NTD per ping)

Explanation:
The fitted line has a negative slope of about -0.73, so house price per unit area
falls as the distance to the nearest MRT station grows. Each extra 100 m lowers the
predicted price by about 0.73 units, or roughly 7,300 New Taiwan dollars per ping.
The intercept of 45.85 is the predicted price for a house right at the station.
At 500 m the model predicts 45.85 - 5 x 0.7262 = 42.22 units. The points are widely
spread around the line, so distance alone does not explain all price variation.

==================================================================
TASK 2 - Compare Models for Yacht Resistance
==================================================================
Results (308 experiments, 5-fold CV, random_state=42):
  Linear    fold RMSEs: 8.16, 10.57, 9.84, 8.38, 7.78   Mean = 8.95
  Quadratic fold RMSEs: 3.93, 4.59, 4.45, 4.49, 3.50    Mean = 4.19
  Selected model: Quadratic (degree-2 polynomial)

Explanation:
The quadratic model is better. Its mean CV RMSE of 4.19 is less than half the linear
model's 8.95, and it wins in every one of the five folds, so the improvement is
consistent. Resistance rises sharply and non-linearly with Froude number (speed), so a
straight line cannot follow the curve, while the squared term lets the fit bend. Both
models used the same folds, so the comparison is fair. The split is row-based, so
experiments from the same hull can fall in both training and validation folds, which
may make the error estimates slightly optimistic.

==================================================================
TASK 3 - Tune a Concrete Strength Model
==================================================================
Results:
  After removing duplicates: 804 training rows, 201 test rows
  Linear baseline mean CV RMSE = 10.20 MPa
  Top 3 settings (mean CV RMSE):
    1. degree=3, alpha=0.1   -> 6.09 MPa
    2. degree=3, alpha=0.01  -> 6.16 MPa
    3. degree=3, alpha=1     -> 6.27 MPa
  Refitted winner on test set: RMSE = 6.52 MPa, MAE = 4.67 MPa

Explanation:
The tuned model (degree 3, alpha 0.1) cuts mean CV RMSE from 10.20 MPa to 6.09 MPa,
an improvement of about 40%. Concrete strength depends on ingredients and curing age
in a non-linear way, so polynomial terms capture patterns a plain linear model misses,
and Ridge keeps the many coefficients from overfitting. The test RMSE of 6.52 MPa is
close to the CV RMSE of 6.09 MPa, so the model generalises well to unseen data.
An example degree-2 interaction term is Cement x Water, which lets the effect of
cement on strength depend on how much water is used. Scaling belongs inside the
pipeline because polynomial features have very different ranges and Ridge penalises
coefficient size. Inside the pipeline the scaler is fitted only on each training
fold, so no information from the validation fold leaks into training.
